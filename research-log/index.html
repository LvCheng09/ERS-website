The Event Reasoning System (ERS) is an exploration of how AI might reason over explicit states, compose reusable operations, and adapt its strategy when evidence or goals change. The research essay explains the motivation, the paper gives the formal arguments, and the technical report describes the released implementation.

This notebook records the questions left open. Its purpose is to make the research process visible: what we believe might work, how we intend to test it, what fails, and which conclusions survive further evidence. Plans and hypotheses appear here before they become verified results.

Current Focus

Our current public baseline is ERS Demo v0.1, an inference implementation for finance and inventory/supply-chain examples. It includes a text-to-Event-Graph frontend, Rule matching, bounded Control decisions, execution, and output checks. Training, Auto-Learn, and a general dependency-invalidation engine are not included in that release.

The next questions concern what the system should learn, how Control should spend a limited reasoning budget, and how conclusions should be maintained as evidence changes. Each needs an explicit experimental design before we can claim improvement.

Status: inference demo public · new training results not yet reported in this notebook.

Ideas & Hypotheses

We use this space for ideas before the experiments are ready. Each note states an intuition, where it might fail, and what observation would change our mind.

Working hypothesis · Not yet experimentally verified

Can a Rule Network decide what it needs to learn next?

An exploration of coverage gaps, uncertainty, conflict, and expected information gain as signals for choosing the next training example or learning task.

Working hypothesis · Multimodal input not in Demo v0.1

Can different modalities share an explicit Event interface?

Why features, object identities, relations, and source qualifications might provide a common interface for text, images, and other signals—and where that assumption can break down.

Experiments & Training

The objective here is reproducible evidence. A training or evaluation record should name the version, dataset, baseline, seed, environment, metrics, failures, and links to raw output. Until such records exist, the items below remain experiment proposals.

Planned experiment · No new results claimed

When is hierarchical Control actually worth its cost?

Design matched-budget tests of flat versus task-organized Control, including candidate evaluations, executed Rules, wall time, CPU use, tokens, and justified refusal.

Planned experiment · Training not released

Does learning order change the Rule Network we obtain?

Compare sequences of examples under fixed data and compute: random order, easy-to-hard, coverage-first, and uncertainty-first—then measure held-out competence and stability.

Planned experiment · Research-only mechanism

What should be recomputed when a premise changes?

Evaluate support-aware revision against full recomputation using shared premises, independent proofs, fact withdrawal, contradictions, and changes in authorization.
